# DNA circle sequencing: published preparation prefix

Lou, Hussmann, McBee et al. (2013), **High-throughput DNA sequencing errors are reduced by orders of magnitude using circle sequencing**, PNAS110,19872–19877. [Defining original](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1319590110). This is DNA circle sequencing, distinct from RNA CirSeq and from vector-cloning CypherSeq.

The defining published article was retrieved from the [UC repository](https://escholarship.org/content/qt3gt268r9/qt3gt268r9_noSplash_fda4f316e06527ded407567209a30186.pdf). Its printed title, DOI and3December2013issue identify the final original. Repository upload/index dates do not change the publication version. Standard ePMC returned500, official PMCCloud supplied no usable original, and the exact supplement index printed in the article returned403. The available final main, not a later revamp or an upstream drawing, supplies this partial preparation.

One original inspected: Results128–177/215–260 and MaterialsMethods499–513, including Fig1B caption. Mandatory oligo scraper after retrieval returned0sequences/0sequence-positive files. That does not establish oligo absence; exact primer and detailed reaction information remain unresolved behind the unread SI. Other results, SI and linked material were not audited.

🟢 Selected source input is S288Cyeast genomicDNA, sheared and selected as a narrow nominal150bp slice from1.5percentlow-melting agarose. Actual sequence/cut coordinates and a perfect gel cutoff are not assigned. Source DNA is phosphorylated and denatured; both original strand populations and any remaining classes stay in the preparation. CircLigaseIIssDNAligase circularizes material at printed300ng/approximately3pmol per20µlreaction. These source quantities are not a predicted count/yield of closed single-strand circles. The exact ends, buffer, times and product population are unassembled.

🟢 Exonuclease processes uncircularizedDNA. Its identity and end specificity are not supplied by the inspected main. The graph retains circular/uncut populations, hydrolysis products and reagents; it does not invent an intervening bead cleanup. Exonuclease-resistant random primers are annealed with variable circle amounts. Their length and resistance chemistry are unassigned: they are not silently called hexamers or phosphorothioate primers.

🟢 Phi29 with dNTPs/BSA/inorganicpyrophosphatase performs strand displacement. Main explicitly says random primers also bind new single-strand products, producing double-stranded tandem-copy material. Original templates, partly copied substrates, primers and all reaction remnants remain in the graph mixture. No exact copy number or strand distribution is predicted.

The selected MaterialsMethods mixture includes UDG and formamidopyrimidine-DNAglycosylase(Fpg) during RCA. Earlier/no-enzyme and conventional-MiSeq treatment comparisons are separate experiments. Results discuss base excision and speculate that damaged templates cease serving as Phi29 substrates. These enzymes are not modeled as faithful U→C or damaged-base restoration, and their actual abasic/end/product inventory and yield remain unresolved. Native lesion chemistry is distinct from the base eventually called by sequencing.

The circle preparation precedes ordinary sequencing-library construction. The main supports MiSeq2×150 and2×250reads, but its exact downstream workup, adapter/junction chemistry, sample indexes and read-primer layout require the SI. The graph therefore ends through an explicitly unassembled library boundary. The sentence that “barcoded samples” were prepared using Schmitt2012 describes the comparison arm; it does not authorize importing the Duplex Sequencing constructor into this circle route or treating molecule barcodes as Illumina sample indexes.

`selected_reference()` reuses the neutral existing single-strand closure/RCA geometry with explicit caller-provided DNA, primer, traversal count and both end flags. It checks a unique exact primer site and derives a selected ideal traversal. Its returned local reference and rendered segments default to inferred. It is not an actual S288Ctemplate, published primer, observed repeat count, random-primer efficiency prediction or validation of polymerase/error statistics. It is not inserted into the source specimen graph.

No numerical error rate, independent-copy statistical guarantee or perfect damage removal is asserted by the model. Published2014comment/reply identities surfaced during discovery—[Schmitt et al.](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1400941111) and [reply](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1402606111)—but their originals remain uninspected.
