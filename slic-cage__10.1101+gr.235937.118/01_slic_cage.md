# SLIC-CAGE

Cvetesic et al.2018, *SLIC-CAGE: high-resolution transcription start site mapping using nanogram-levels of total RNA*, [DOI10.1101/gr.235937.118](https://doi.org/10.1101/gr.235937.118).

This is a capped source-qualified note and private incomplete graph. Two published originals were inspected: VORPMC6280763 Methods169–201, and the published Supplemental Material, selected TablesS1/S2/S5/S6 plus FigS20. Original TableS6p8 and FigS20p52 were rendered and read. The mandatory oligo scraper was rerun after text conversion; its sequence hits were followed to the source. Automatically discovered preprint368795 is superseded and excluded. The downloaded processing-code archive and unrelated result tables were not chemically inspected.

🟢 The manufactured carrier is distinct from biological RNA of interest. Source PCR creates variable-length templates from a synthetic gene in pJ241; T7 IVT, purification and partial capping produce the carrier stock. I-SceI/I-CeuI recognition sequences are embedded in the synthetic gene so that later DNA copies can be selectively cut. PCR templates have five random5′ and six random3′ nucleotides; identifier function is unassigned, and these are not called UMIs. The386–1034bp range refers to PCR templates, not asserted transcript lengths. Upstream complete plasmid/PCR/IVT/cap chemistry remains unassembled. Capping-efficiency polyphosphatase/Terminator assays are diagnostic and do not replace the production stock.

🟢 Main Methods specify a nominal90%uncapped/10%capped carrier mixture. TableS5 gives approximately44µg uncapped plus5µg capped, described as approximately50µg mix. These are recipe mass descriptions, not exact fractions of capped molecules. The local graph uses the source’s illustrative10ng RNA plus4990ng carrier recipe, without assigning a specimen or assuming its full cap composition.

The source explicitly follows Murata2014 nAnT-iCAGE for RT/cap-trapping/library steps. That original chapter has not been read in this pass, and no existing CAGE constructor is imported. The source-qualified boundary remains opaque. Child-specific exceptions are retained: libraries are kept separate until sequencing, samples are never completely dried and redissolved, and the final inherited AMPure product is concentrated/adjusted to15µl. The initial Bioanalyzer consumes1µl, leaving14µl for carrier digestion. A molecule’s classification as carrier does not itself imply that every recovered library fragment has a cuttable site or that no biological target contains such a site.

🟢 First I-SceI/I-CeuI treatment is37°C3h followed by65°C20min heat inactivation. Cut fragments, uncut/partial molecules and enzymes remain until actual1.8×AMPure purification. Source42µl elution is concentrated to20µl. Separate whole-library and carrier-selective KAPA SYBR qPCR assays use1µl input per reaction; technical replicate/scaling details are unassigned. The production PCR receives its own18µl allocation, both adapter primers and KAPA HiFi HS ReadyMix. Its cycle count follows whole-library Ct rather than a universal fixed count. Diagnostic products never become production template.

🟢 Production PCR is followed by1.8×AMPure cleanup,42µl elution and concentration to14µl. A second carrier digestion is followed by stringent1×AMPure purification,42µl elution and concentration to12µl. Bioanalyzer measurements decide whether an additional0.8×AMPure step is needed for fragments below300bp, with repeat assessment. The default graph leaves that outcome unassigned; explicit positive/negative branches preserve whether this physical separation occurs. Final leftover-carrier qPCR and FigS20 PicoGreen measurements consume independent analytical aliquots. Only then are independently prepared libraries pooled. Source-read filtering does not physically remove DNA.
