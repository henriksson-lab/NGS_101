# ESEGA — selected single-stranded ZP1 preparation

Defining publication: [Enzyme-assisted high throughput sequencing of an expanded genetic alphabet at single base resolution](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-48408-9), 2024.

This private partial model uses the published main article, especially 129–145, 233–251 and 277–295, and published Supplementary Information Table 1 (original p5; sequence and red barcode annotations inspected) and Table 2 identity/p6. Two originals were counted before opening; the source investigation is closed. The mandatory scraper's hits were checked against those originals. No preprint, peer-review file, reporting summary or Source Data workbook supplies chemistry here. This is not a complete linked-source or missing-information review.

🟢 ESEGA sequences synthetic DNA containing Z and P using differential APOBEC treatment and a nonstandard PCR nucleotide pool. The selected source route is single-stranded ZP1, deaminated, with the five-triphosphate readout discussed in Results/Fig3. Untreated controls, four-triphosphate readout, double-strand separation and upstream six-triphosphate synthesis remain distinct source branches. The model does not infer that every molecule takes the intended path.

🟢 Main247 denatures 20µl 1µM DNA with 4µl formamide at85°C10min, then ice; adds100µl premix containing APOBEC and incubates37°C3h. Bead cleanup, column cleanup and direct heat inactivation90°C60s are alternative continuations. The model selects direct heat, then the source dilution and1µl transfer. It does not insert an extra cleanup or assume complete conversion/complete template removal.

🟢 Main251 gives four-triphosphate PCR as0.2mM each A/C/G/T, and the five-triphosphate pool as those four plus0.05mM dZTP, without dPTP. The upstream six-triphosphate synthesis pool is a separate experiment. P supports first-round Z incorporation; subsequent copying can yield G. Expected synthetic Z:P readout is C:G, while deaminated ordinary C:G can be read T:A. Chemical U and PCR-reported T are distinct. Source measurements do not establish a deterministic complete transformation of every molecule.
