## Molecular path

🟢 A sequenced puck supplies spatial barcode oligos through a photocleavable linker. Released oligos enter nearby nuclei before dissociation. Standard 10x processing supplies a cell barcode; a feature-barcode-style library connects that cell barcode to one or more spatial barcodes.

🟢 The protocol supports snRNA-seq and joint snRNA/snATAC. The published Methods distinguish 10x 3′ v3.1 snRNA processing from 10x Multiome processing (JATS lines 238–243; Supplementary Methods 1.5). The graph now branches before Multiome Tn5 tagmentation and GEM partitioning: 3′ RNA and spatial-tag products never pass through Tn5, while Multiome RNA, ATAC and spatial-tag products do. It records the Multiome spatial-tag library as a sequenced endpoint with an unresolved junction and a visible validation warning; the source prints its spike-in and custom P5/P7 PCR primers, but the checked sources do not yet establish a complete strand-level construct. The source names a NextSeq 1000 P2 100-cycle kit and optional NovaSeq resequencing, not a branch-specific read-cycle recipe; the graph does not assert individual read lengths.

## Source-printed oligos

🟢 The published Supplementary Methods specify three puck designs. `PC` denotes the photocleavable linker, `N` a mixed random base and `V` a non-T base under IUPAC notation. Its cited Slide-seqV2 predecessor defines each `J` as a split-pool barcode base shared by oligos on one bead. The directly synthesized capture oligo is printed as `5′-TTT-PC-GTGACTGGAGTTCAGACGTGTGCTCTTCCGATCTJJJJJJJJTCTTCAGCGTTCCCGAGAJJJJJJJNNNNNNNVVGCTTTAAGGCCGGTCCTAGCAA-3′`. Its poly(A) variant ends after `NNNNNNNVV` in `A30`. For the ligated puck design, the source prints a different 5′ arm and the exact extension oligo `GAGCTTTGCTAACGGTCGAGGCTTTAAGGCCGGTCCTAGCAA`, annealed with the splint shown 3′→5′ as `CTGTGTACCCGCCTCGAAACGATTGC`.

🟢 The full ligated-design bead oligo in MOESM1 is `5′-TTT_PC_GCCGGTAATACGACTCACTATAGGGCTACACGACGCTCTTCCGATCTJJJJJJJJTCTTCAGCGTTCCCGAGAJJJJJJJNNNNNNNVVGCTCGGACACATGGGCG-3′`. The main article PDF and JATS instead print `TTT_PC_zCCGG...`; this is an apparent discrepancy **within the published sources**. The model displays the supplement's base-only form because `z` is not defined as a nucleotide or modification in these Methods. Its intended meaning in the main article remains uncertain.

🟢 The Multiome branch adds spike-in primer `GTGACTGGAGTTCAGACGT`. Spatial-tag PCR uses `STAG_iP7_a1`, `CAAGCAGAAGACGGCATACGAGATATTTACCGCAGTGACTGGAGTTCAGACGT*G*T`, and `P5-STAG_ip5_a1`, `AATGATACGGCGACCACCGAGATCTACACGACAATAAAGACACTCTTTCCCTACACGACGC*T*C`; asterisks mark the printed terminal phosphorothioate bonds. The 3′ RNA branch instead uses the named `STAG_P701_NEX` and `P5-Truseq Hybrid` primers, whose bases are not printed in these Methods passages.

🟢 The cited CG000317 Rev C Appendix p. 71 prints the 3′ v3.1 RNA bead primer `CTACACGACGCTCTTCCGATCT-N16-N12-T30VN` and feature-barcode capture bead primer `GTCAGATGTGTATAAGAGACAG-N16-N12-TTGCTAGGACCGGCCTTAAAGC`. CG000338 Rev F Appendix p. 81 prints the same fixed RNA scaffold plus the Multiome ATAC primer `AATGATACGGCGACCACCGAGATCTACAC-N16-CGCGTCTG` and final ATAC/GEX library structures. `N16` and `N12` are variable barcode and UMI windows, not undisclosed fixed sequence.

🟡 The public graph keeps variable cell and puck barcodes as roles, and does not yet reconstruct every branch-specific strand junction. This is a modeling limit, not a claim that the fixed commercial oligos are unavailable.

🟢 The [author spatial parser](https://github.com/broadchenf/Slide-tags/blob/main/cell_barcode_matcher.R) assigns `R1_file_path` to an internal variable named `R2` and `R2_file_path` to `R1` (lines 8–9, 30–41). It extracts a 16-base cell barcode and 12-base feature UMI from positions 1–16 and 17–28 of the cell-barcode input (lines 55–59, 135–139), and reconstructs a 14-base puck barcode from positions 1–8 and 27–32 of the spatial input (lines 135–164). The 12-base UMI agrees with CG000317 Rev C. The printed puck oligos contain two `J` blocks of 8 and 7 positions (15 total), while this parser consumes 8 and 6 positions (14 total); the checked sources do not yet establish how the remaining printed position maps to a sequenced read. Earlier model values of UMI10 and a contiguous 20-base puck barcode were unsupported. The 3′ feature-library example now shows the guide's published Nextera Read 1N shell and separate 8-base and 6-base puck identifier pieces with 18 intervening read bases; branch-specific FASTQ read lengths remain unspecified.

🟡 The final panel places those read-observed puck pieces into a linear 3′ feature-library example. The exact strand-level junction of the custom puck payload and the separate Multiome spatial-tag shell are not reconstructed from the checked sources; the panel marks that placement inferred.
