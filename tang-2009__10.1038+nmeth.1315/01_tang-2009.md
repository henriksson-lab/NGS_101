## 1. What it is

One mouse cell (a 4-cell-stage blastomere, or a mature oocyte, including *Dicer1*⁻/⁻ and
*Ago2*⁻/⁻ oocytes) is mouth-pipetted into a tube of lysis buffer that already contains the
oligo-dT RT primer. 🟢 Lysis, RT, primer removal and poly(A) tailing all happen in that one
tube; the tailed product is then split into four PCR tubes that are pooled after the first
PCR (steps 24–34). 🟢

The front end is the **Kurimoto/Saitou single-cell cDNA amplification** (refs 38–39 of the
protocol), which the authors say they "improved" 🟢 (exactly what changed is not itemised
in the text read; the 6-min + 6 s/cycle extension is one visible feature 🟡): oligo-dT RT → **Exonuclease I** to
destroy unused primer → **TdT poly(A) tailing** of the first strand (with RNase H) →
second strand primed by a *second* oligo-dT primer on that A tail → PCR with both primers.
There is no template switching, no cell barcode, no UMI. The back end is a standard
**SOLiD fragment library**: shear, end-repair, ligate P1/P2 adaptors, nick-translate.

| | New thing here | Where else it turns up |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Whole-transcriptome **sequencing** (not microarray) of one cell — 0.5–3 kb cDNA, sheared into a shotgun library | every later full-length method (SMART-seq, SMART-seq2) replaces the TdT step with [template switching](../ref/concepts/template-switching.md) |
| 2 | **TdT poly(A) tail + second oligo-dT primer** (UP2) to put a handle on the 3' end of the first strand | Kurimoto 2006 (the source of the front end); contrast [reverse transcription](../ref/concepts/reverse-transcription.md) with TSO |
| 3 | **5'-amine-blocked** amplification primers (AUP1/AUP2) so the cDNA *ends* cannot ligate to the sequencing adaptors after shearing | the same "block the ends" trick recurs where end fragments would dominate |
| 4 | 16 **barcoded SOLiD P2 adaptors** for 16 cells per run (protocol paper only) | — |

Stated limits 🟢 (Nat Protoc introduction): poly(A)+ RNA only; **not strand-specific**;
only about **3 kb from the 3' end** is captured (≈36 % of genes are longer, so their 5'
parts are missed). Supplementary Fig. 9 shows the same 3' coverage limit.

### How they interlock — 🟡 (computed)

**cDNA primers.**

- UP1 and UP2 are each a **24-nt handle + T₂₄** (48 nt). No `VN` anchor, so priming
  is not pinned to the poly(A)/transcript junction (see
  [reverse transcription](../ref/concepts/reverse-transcription.md)).
- The handles carry restriction sites: UP1 `ATAT` · **BamHI** `GGATCC` · **AscI**
  `GGCGCGCC` · **SalI** `GTCGAC`; UP2 `ATAT` · **XhoI** `CTCGAG` · AscI · BamHI. The two
  handles share only `ATAT`, a central 9-nt `GGCGCGCCG` and their last base (`C`); they are different
  handles, so the amplicon is asymmetric (UP1 at the first-strand end, UP2 at the
  second-strand end). The sites are a relic of the cloning-era design; nothing in this
  protocol cuts them. 🔴 (purpose not stated)
- AUP1 / AUP2 are UP1 / UP2 with a 5' amine — identical bases.

**SOLiD adaptors.**

- Fragment P1: lower strand = revcomp(upper, 41 nt) + `TT` (43 nt). P2: upper
  (25 nt) = revcomp(lower, 23 nt) + `TT`. So each adaptor is blunt at the insert end and
  has a 3' **TT overhang at the distal end**, which keeps adaptors from ligating to each
  other end-to-end there.
- An adaptor dimer spans 2 + 41 + 23 + 2 = **68 nt** — the "68-bp primer-dimer" that the
  protocol says AMPure removes after ligation.
- **Short (barcode-kit) P1**: `P1-Adaptor-3-end` (23 nt) is the **3' 23 nt of the
  41-nt P1 upper strand** (it starts at position 19); `P1-Adaptor-5-end` = its
  revcomp + `TT`. The 41-nt *16Barcode Library PCR Primer-1* is identical to the full P1
  upper strand, i.e. **18 extra nt (`CCACTACGCCTCCGCTTT`) + the short P1**: PCR with it
  rebuilds the full P1. The older 28-nt Fragment PCR Primer 1 would overlap the short P1
  by only 10 nt, which is presumably why the barcode workflow uses a longer primer.
- **Barcoded P2**: upper = `CGCCTTGGCCGTACAGCAG` (19 nt, an internal adaptor) + bc (6) +
  the 25-nt fragment-P2 upper strand; lower = the 23-nt fragment-P2 lower strand +
  revcomp(bc) + revcomp(internal adaptor). Lower = revcomp(upper) minus the `TT`, for all
  16. The barcodes are 16 distinct 6-mers, minimum pairwise Hamming distance **2**,
  GC 50–83 %.
- Library PCR Primer 2 (21 nt) is the first 21 nt of the P2 lower strand; its revcomp
  `AGAATGAGGAACCCGGGGCAG` is in the P2 upper strand, so the amplified library ends
  `…AGAGAATGAGGAACCCGGGGCAG` and the `TT` is lost.

## 3. Step by step (Nat Protoc 2010)

Conditions 🟢 from the protocol; "first strand / second strand" structures 🟡 unless the
protocol states them.

**cDNA (one cell per reaction; split into four tubes at step 5)**

1. **Lysis**: 4.45 µL lysis buffer — 0.9× PCR Buffer II, 1.35 mM MgCl₂, 0.45 % NP-40,
   4.5 mM DTT, SUPERase-In + RNase inhibitor, **12.5 nM UP1**, 0.045 mM each dNTP (final
   in the 5 µL RT). Pick one cell (with ~0.1 µL PBS-BSA); 70 °C 90 s, ice. A picking-buffer
   blank is mandatory as negative control.
2. **RT**: + 0.45 µL SuperScript III (13.2 U/µL final), RNase inhibitor, **T4 gene 32
   protein**; 50 °C 30 min, 70 °C 10 min. First strand: `5'-UP1 handle · T24 · <antisense cDNA>-3'`.
3. **Free-primer removal**: Exonuclease I, 37 °C 30 min, 80 °C 25 min. The protocol states
   that this destroys free UP1 and also **shortens cDNA 3' ends by about 50 nt**, while the
   5' UP1 end stays intact. 🟢
4. **Poly(A) tailing**: + 6 µL with **3 mM dATP, TdT** (0.75 U/µL) and **RNase H**; 37 °C
   15 min, 70 °C 10 min. First strand becomes `5'-UP1 · T24 · <cDNA> · A(n)-3'`.
5. **Second strand**: split into 4 × 3 µL; + 19 µL Ex Taq HS mix with **UP2** (1 µM);
   one cycle 95 °C 3 min, 50 °C 2 min, 72 °C 10 min. The protocol writes the product as
   `5'-UP2-(T)n-cDNA-(A)n-UP1-3'` 🟢 (here "UP1" means its complement).
6. **PCR 1**: + 19 µL mix with **UP1** (final UP1 = UP2 = 0.46 µM); 95 °C 3 min; **20
   cycles** (18 for an oocyte) of 95 °C 30 s, 67 °C 1 min, 72 °C 6 min **+6 s per cycle**.
   Pool the 4 tubes; QIAquick.
7. **PCR 2**: 4 × 90 µL with **AUP1 + AUP2** (1 µM each), 1.2 µL template, **9 cycles**
   (6–12 allowed; total 1st + 2nd < 30) with the same program. QIAquick.
8. **Gel**: 1 % agarose, excise **0.5–3 kb**, gel-extract, Qubit. ≥100 ng expected from a
   blastomere.

Amplified cDNA 🟡:

```
5'- (NH2)AUP2 handle · T24 · <cDNA, sense> · A24 · UP1 handle' -3'
```

with both 5' ends amine-blocked.

**SOLiD library (Express protocol)**

9. **Anneal adaptors**: equal volumes of 1 mM oligos in 1× ligase buffer; 95 °C 5 min, then
   72/60/50/40/30/20/10 °C steps, 4 °C.
10. **Shear**: 1–2 µg in 100 µL, Covaris S2 (20 % duty, intensity 5, 200 cycles/burst,
    60 s, 15 cycles) → mean **100–110 bp**.
11. **End repair**: End-It (Epicentre), RT 30 min, 65 °C 20 min. Fragments now blunt and
    5'-phosphorylated 🟡 (End-It contains T4 PNK; not stated).
12. **Ligate**: P1 (ds) + P2 (ds) adaptors at 30× molar excess over fragments, NEB Quick
    Ligase, RT 10–15 min; **one barcoded P2 per sample**. AMPure 1.8×.
    The amine-blocked cDNA ends cannot ligate 🟢 (footnote / critical step); adaptors
    land only on internal fragments.
13. **Pool** barcoded samples at equal concentration (Qubit or qPCR), AMPure.
14. **Nick translation**: DNA Pol I, NEBuffer 2, 16 °C 30 min; EDTA; AMPure. The adaptor
    strands that face the insert 5' end are not phosphorylated, so one nick per end
    remains after ligation and Pol I moves it out 🟡 (standard SOLiD reasoning; the
    protocol gives only the step).
15. **qPCR** with SYBR against an *E. coli* DH10B standard library (62 °C anneal). qPCR
    primer sequences not given 🔴.
16. **Optional PCR** (only if < 1 ng/µL): Platinum SuperMix + Pfu + AmpliTaq with
    **16Barcode Library PCR Primer 1 + 2**; 95 °C 5 min, **8–16 cycles** of 95 °C 15 s,
    62 °C 15 s, 70 °C 1 min; 70 °C 5 min. AMPure.
17. Emulsion PCR and SOLiD sequencing (not described in the protocol).

The 2009 paper (Nat Methods) presumably used the same cDNA amplification — its
Supplementary Fig. 1 shows a 0.5–3 kb cDNA smear from single oocytes, as the protocol's
Fig. 1 does after 20 + 9 cycles — and unbarcoded SOLiD fragment libraries. Supplementary Fig. 5 shows two libraries made from
the same cDNA correlate at R ≈ 0.996–0.997. 🟡 (2009 methods not read)

## 4. Final libraries — 🟡 (assembled from the oligos above)

**2009, unbarcoded** (one cell per library):

```
5'- P1 (41: CCACTACGCCTCCGCTTTCCTCTCTATGGGCAGTCGGTGAT) · <cDNA fragment, either orientation> · P2' (23: AGAGAATGAGGAACCCGGGGCAG) -3'
```

64 nt of adaptor + insert (~100–110 bp sheared).

**2010, 16-plex** (after the PCR in step 16):

```
5'- P1 (41) · <cDNA fragment> · internal adaptor (19: CGCCTTGGCCGTACAGCAG) · <bc, 6> · P2' (23: AGAGAATGAGGAACCCGGGGCAG) -3'
```

89 nt of adaptor + insert. If the optional PCR is skipped, the P1 end is only the 23-nt
short P1 (and the P2 end keeps its `TT`); whether emulsion PCR works from that is not
discussed. 🔴

Because adaptors ligate to blunt fragments without orientation, either cDNA strand can sit
next to P1 → **unstranded** (matches the stated limitation 🟢).

## 5. Sequencing

🟢 Applied Biosystems **SOLiD** reads of **35 or 50
bases** (colour space — the supplementary mapping pipeline works on `csfasta` colour reads;
single-end is assumed, no paired reads are mentioned 🟡) (supplementary table headers "35-mer"/"50-mer"; Supplementary Fig. 3 plots error
rate over the first 44 positions of 50-mer reads), **≈350 million reads** in total over all
cells (Supplementary Fig. 2), 66–81 % mapping uniquely.

Read layout 🟡: the tag read is primed from the P1 adaptor and reads into the cDNA
fragment. In the 16-plex design the 6-nt barcode is read separately, from a primer on the
19-nt internal adaptor (barcode read). The SOLiD sequencing-primer sequences (tag and
barcode) are not given in any source read 🔴; the upstream page does not give them either.

## 6. Open questions

- 🔴 Which **P1 adaptor** goes into the barcoded ligation: Table 1 lists both the full
  41/43-nt fragment P1 and a short 23/25-nt "P1-Adaptor"; the procedure says only "P1 (ds)".
  The 41-nt barcode PCR primer-1 (= full P1) is consistent with the short one.
- 🔴 The **Fragment Library P2 PCR Primer** cell is empty in the PMC table. The 2009 table's
  "Library PCR Primer 2" `CTGCCCCGGGTTCCTCATTCT` fills it, and is identical to
  16Barcode Library PCR Primer-2 🟡.
- 🔴 Purpose of the restriction sites in the UP handles; whether the 2009 paper used exactly
  the 2010 cycle numbers / volumes (2009 Online Methods not read).
- 🔴 SOLiD read and barcode-read primer sequences; barcode read length.
- 🟡 The naming of the short P1 oligos in Table 1 is inverted relative to the fragment P1
  pair (`P1-Adaptor-5-end` is the `ATCACC…GGTT` strand, whereas `…P1 Adaptor -5'end` is
  the `CCACTAC…` strand). Sequences are unambiguous; the names are not.

## 8. How this note was made (tool evaluation)

`tools/get_sources.py` fetched the upstream page, all ten Nat Methods supplementary files
and the Nat Protoc full text from PMC; the Nat Methods article itself was not fetched
(paywall). The supplementary **oligo table is an old binary `.xls`**, for which
`get_sources.py` made no `.txt` twin and `tools/doctext.py` refused ("binary, not a format
this reads"); it was converted by hand with LibreOffice
(`soffice --headless --convert-to csv`, cp1252 → UTF-8) to
`nmeth.1315_…_MOESM306_ESM.xls.txt`. `tools/scrape_primers.py` then found the Table 1
oligos in the PMC text and the upstream page, but most of its 80 hits were TaqMan
validation primers/probes from the junction-validation table (MOESM302), which are not library
chemistry. The spaced triplets in the `.xls` (`ATA TGG ATC …`) and its 3'→5'-written lower
strands are not picked up as oligos; `--find` matched the spaced upper strands but cannot
match a strand written 3'→5'. The Nat Protoc table's empty "Fragment Library P2 PCR
Primer" cell is a genuine gap in the PMC HTML, not a conversion loss.
