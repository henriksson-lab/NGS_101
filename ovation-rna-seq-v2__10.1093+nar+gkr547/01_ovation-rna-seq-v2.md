## Molecular workflow

🟢 First strand: “a combination of random hexamers and poly-T chimeric primer” with
reverse transcriptase (Introduction line 26; Methods line 56).

🟢 Second strand: “the RNA template is partially degraded in a heating step and the
second strand is synthesized along the first-strand cDNA as template using DNA
polymerase” (Introduction line 26). The Methods (line 56) instead say the double-stranded
DNA “is generated by fragmentation of the mRNA template strand using RNA-dependant DNA
polymerase”. The model follows the Introduction and shows both statements as a published
discrepancy on the page. 🟡 No separate second-strand primer is described, so none is
drawn (an earlier model version had an unsupported chimeric second-strand primer).

🟢 The double-stranded DNA is purified with Agencourt RNAClean XP beads (Methods line 56).

🟢 SPIA: RNase H degrades the RNA in the DNA/RNA heteroduplex at the 5′ end of the
double-stranded cDNA, the SPIA primer binds and the polymerase extends it, displacing the
existing forward strand. “Finally, random hexamers were used to amplify the second-strand
cDNA linearly” (lines 26 and 56), giving the double-stranded cDNA used for libraries.

🟢 Library (line 60): ~0.5–1 µg double-stranded cDNA (from 100 ng total RNA). Libraries
were made from sheared cDNA (Covaris S1, duty cycle 5, intensity 3, 200 cycles/burst,
180 s; 100–360 bp), unsheared cDNA (100–550 bp) or an equal mix. After shearing, cDNA was
run on a 2.5% agarose gel and 250–350 bp was size-selected for Illumina. Fragments were
end-repaired, dA-tailed (Illumina libraries) and ligated to platform-specific
double-stranded bar-coded adapters from New England Biolabs kits, with automated
purification; gel purification and 15-cycle library amplification were manual. The page
draws the sheared branch; the paper gives the order of the two manual steps only as
“gel purification and library amplification”.

🟢 Sequencing: Genome Analyzer IIx paired-end, 2 × 50 bases (line 108; Supplementary
Figure 1). The eight mouse libraries were also sequenced single-read on SOLiD (not drawn).

## Final library and reads

🟡 The endpoint uses inferred native NEB adapter regions, with unknown barcode encoding
and placement, rather than a canonical TruSeq shell. The inspected article establishes
bar-coded adapters and paired 2 × 50 reads. Exact adapter strands, primer sites, barcode
position/width and index acquisition remain unassigned. An unknown index-read assignment
is not evidence that index reads were absent.

## Native graph migration (2026-10-18, batch03)

Re-read the published PMC3185437 JATS Introduction 26, Methods 56/60/64 and
Results 108 (one original); ran the mandatory source-bundle scraper (zero hits,
not an absence finding). The current graph owns the selected sheared Illumina
branch. RNA template and first-strand cDNA, opposite DNA strands and displaced SPIA
copies are separate rows, never covalently concatenated populations. The primer-derived
RNA region is attached to the first-strand lineage; exact heteroduplex pairing remains
unassigned. Heat/RNase H products have unresolved physical fates; hydrolysis does not
physically discard them. Random-hexamer second-strand products are duplexes. End-repaired
and dA-tailed states are separate, with opposed exposed dA ends computed from complementary
segments. Native ligation, purification and gel states feed PCR directly; the final
PCR duplex is not substituted for that precursor. The listed manual gel-before-PCR
order is a selected schematic order, explicitly unresolved experimentally in this scope.

The previous canonical adapter shell, default index position and implied acquired index
read were model assumptions, not publication errors. They have been removed. The page
retains the published Introduction/Methods second-strand wording discrepancy. Commercial
V2 guide M01206, Kurn 2005, Dafforn 2004, exact historical NEB adapters/primers, unsheared,
mixed and SOLiD branches remain deferred. This is a bounded migration, not acceptance of
missing-information claims or proof of native commercial chemistry.
