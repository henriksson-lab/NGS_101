# RF cloning — illustrative linear copying

[van den Ent and Lowe2006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbbm.2005.12.008), “RF cloning: A restriction-free method for inserting target genes into plasmids”. The [author-hosted published original](https://www2.mrc-lmb.cam.ac.uk/groups/JYL/PDF/rfcloning2006%20published.pdf) was read: Methods2.1/2.2, Results and Figure1, printed pp68–71. This is the original RF branch, distinct from EMP and exponential whole-plasmid PCR.

## Scope and source mechanism

This is an **invented illustrative cloning example**, not a published experimental instance. The short DNA maps and primers are neutral model choices with no replicon or functional gene. No usable vector, biological function, recovery, efficiency or yield is predicted.

🟢 The article first makes a tailed insert PCR product using KOD hot-start polymerase and purifies it. This duplex product acts as a primer pair for linear copying of a circular recipient using PfuTurbo. The two ends match recipient windows flanking the replaced interval. Figure1 describes the product as a double-nicked circular duplex. DpnI treatment targets methylated parent DNA; the mixture is then transformed into cells. The article distinguishes linear copying from exponential amplification and discusses displacement-derived alternatives. The example keeps that distinction without assigning their frequency.
